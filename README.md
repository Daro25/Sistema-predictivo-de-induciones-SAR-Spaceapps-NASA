SPILL NASA

We developed SPILL NASA, a real-time flood monitoring and early prediction mobile application specifically engineered to protect communities in the Álamo Temapache region of Veracruz, Mexico. Our system integrates Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) data processing from the Sentinel-1 satellite alongside key hydrometeorological variables, including accumulated precipitation, maximum humidity, and water volume. By leveraging SAR microwave imaging, SPILL NASA penetrates dense cloud cover and operates continuously day and night during severe storms, accurately mapping water extent and soil saturation when traditional optical sensors are blinded. Through an intuitive interface featuring a dynamic predictive system, historical radar queries, and a standardized early warning traffic-light system (Normal, Precaution, Alert, and Danger/Evacuation), the tool translates complex geospatial data into accessible, actionable intelligence for both citizens and Civil Protection authorities. This approach democratizes orbital-grade Earth observation, reducing response times to local basin overflows and strengthening community resilience against hydrometeorological disasters.
